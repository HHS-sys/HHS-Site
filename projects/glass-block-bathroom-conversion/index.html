A Westmount bathroom rebuilt behind the finish

What began as a jetted-tub-to-shower conversion became a deeper rebuild when demolition exposed an unusual combination of concealed conditions. The issues were documented, the appropriate specialists were brought in and the room was completed with a new shower system, tile, fixtures and carefully blended finishes.

The goal

Replace the oversized tub with a brighter, more useful shower

The original jetted tub and deep tiled platform filled the window end of the bathroom. The plan removed that assembly, retained the glass-block window and rebuilt the footprint as a tiled shower with a sliding glass enclosure.

Once the wall and floor were opened, the project needed more than a fixture swap. Each concealed condition was documented and handled before the new wet-area assembly and final finishes went in.

  • Jetted tub and tiled-platform removal
  • Concealed-condition documentation and trade coordination
  • Backing, support and insulation restoration
  • Shower preparation and water management
  • Tile, enclosure, vanity, toilet and fixtures
  • Blended drywall, new trim and repainting

What demolition revealed

An unusual combination, handled one issue at a time

These conditions were unusual and are not what every bathroom renovation uncovers. The important part was to pause in the affected areas, document what was visible and resolve each concern before closing the assembly.

Concealed electrical conditions

Hidden junction boxes and wiring were documented and referred to the appropriate licensed electrical trade before the wall was closed.

Window-wall support and insulation

Missing backing and support around part of the existing window assembly, along with areas without insulation, were restored as the surrounding wall was rebuilt.

A paper-wasp nest in the wall

A large nest was found inside the opened cavity. Work paused in that area while a pest-control specialist handled it safely.

The complete room, not only the shower

The finished scope brought together shower preparation and waterproofing, tile, glass, vanity, toilet, plumbing connections, blended drywall, new trim and paint.

Hekman documented the concealed conditions, coordinated access and completed the surrounding bathroom rebuild. A pest-control specialist handled the paper-wasp nest, and the electrical condition was referred to the appropriate licensed electrical trade before the assembly was closed.
